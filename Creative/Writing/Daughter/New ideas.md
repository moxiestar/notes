# **Outline**

Early 2000s, Pacific Northwest. Diana Kowalski, a young detective at a small-town police station, is going through her ex’s stuff after their long-term relationship has fallen apart. A successful businessman, he has cut her off and moved to the city, leaving her to rebuild her life alone. Her high school friend, Katherine, encourages her to date around town, but Diana is uninterested.

Bill Scofield, once a successful physician-scientist, has relocated to the same small town after his life in the city fell apart. His clinical trials were shut down over misleading results, and during their divorce, his wife Allison won full custody of their daughter Sara. Scofield now lives alone and works at the town clinic, where he is unpopular and largely ignored. He refuses to go to a therapist, disavowing psychology as a field, and with no other way to cope, he turns to alcohol.

While at the bar on a weekend, Diana introduces herself to Scofield. They hit it off, and begin having secret one-night-stands. Over time, they agree to keep things casual. Though Diana shares her angst about her relationship falling apart, Scofield is cagey and vague about his past. After a fight, they call things off.

One evening after work, Scofield gets a call from a hospital in the city, informing him of his wife’s death. As a result, his daughter, now teenage and estranged, is placed under his custody. When she arrives, she refuses to speak to him. Scofield tries to find out more details about Allison’s illness, but he cannot access her records.

Struggling with depression, Diana begins to distance herself from the people her life. When she runs into her ex’s family around town, they make snarky comments about him being better off without her. She talks to the police captain at the station late at night, revealing that her father abandoned her as a child and her mother, an addict, has been in a facility since she was a teenager. She tells the police captain that her ex and his family made comments about her turning out like her mother, something that scares her more and more as she gets older. He insists that she is headed for something better, but she says her life has only gotten worse. At home, Diana has a vision of her mother speaking to her in the mirror.

Sara resents Scofield, and purposefully torments him around the house, messing with his belongings and furniture until he begins to lose the edge. They get into a nasty fight, which ends in Sara running away after Scofield leaves for work in the morning.

The next morning, Diana blows up at a colleague in front of everyone, leading to her dismissal from her current case. She calls Scofield, distraught, and he comes over to her apartment to comfort her. He tells her about his past marriage, his wife’s death, and his struggles with his daughter. Diana is angry, but understanding, and tells him she wants to keep seeing him.

When Scofield gets home, he finds the house empty, with only a short note from Sara explaining her choice to leave. Panicked, he calls Diana and the police, and spends the night driving around town searching for her. He finds her on the side of the road, bleeding and exhausted, and they share their first hug. With the police in tow, he drives her back to the hospital.

Sara recovers, and slowly warms up to Scofield and Diana. At the end of their first year together, Scofield takes Sara ice-skating, once her favorite pastime, and they laugh about their lives. Diana, now living with them, picks them up for dinner at Sara’s favorite restaurant.
