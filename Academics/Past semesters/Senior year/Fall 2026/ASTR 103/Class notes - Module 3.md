## **Lecture Sixteen | Doppler Shift**

**Doppler Shift**

- **Definition**

  - The change in wavelength seen by an observer when a light source is moving either closer or further away
  - The doppler shift can be used to determine an object’s speed, but not its distance
    - If an object is moving side to side across an FOV, a doppler shift will not occur

- **Shifting**

  - An object is **blueshifting** when it moves towards us
  - An object is **redshifting** when it moves away from us

- **Stars**

  - Star color does not change based on movement/distance, since their speed is too slow

  - However, the **lines on the star’s absorption spectrum will shift**

    - If the star blueshifts, the absorption lines will move left

    - If the star redshifts, the absorption lines will move right

## **Lectures Seventeen & Eighteen | Extrasolar planets**

**Exoplanets**

- **Definition:** planets that orbit stars other than the Sun
  - Over 5000 exoplanets have been found
- **Detection methods**
  - Radial velocity method
  - Transit method
  - Direct imaging
  - Gravitational microlensing

**Star and Planet Orbits**

- **A planet and its star both orbit around a common point (the system’s center of mass)**
  - The star and planet are always located on opposite sides of the center of mass
  - They always take the same amount of time to complete one orbit
  - In orbit, their velocities around the center of mass are parallel but directionally opposite
- **Shifting**
  - As the star moves **towards** and **away** from Earth, its light is **blueshifted**, unshifted, and **redshifted**
  - **Note that on spectral graphs…**
    - **Radial velocity:** the part of the star’s spectral graph that points towards or away from us
    - **Transverse velocity:** the part of the star’s spectral graph that does not point towards or away from us
- **Radial velocity curves**
  - A star’s radial velocity is its speed moving towards or away from Earth
  - A radial velocity curve is a graph of the star’s radial velocity over time
    - Negative velocities represent blueshifted light
    - Positive velocities represent redshifted light
  - The time it takes to complete one full radial velocity curve is equal to the orbital period of both the star and the planet
  - **The height of a radial velocity curve corresponds to the amount of Doppler Shift**
    - **Factors influencing the amount of Doppler Shift**
      - The mass of the star
      - The mass of the planet
      - The distance between the star and the planet

**Light curves**

- Graphs that show a star’s brightness over time
